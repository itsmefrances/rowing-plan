What progresses, and what deliberately doesn’t

three tiers

You called this right. For a 500m and a 1-minute, peak power is force × velocity — and past a point the gains come from the velocity end, from rate of force development, not from a bigger 1RM. So load progression is confined to the movements that hold up the force end. The power work progresses in output, where adding weight would actively defeat the purpose. Everything else is held on purpose, because accessory PRs in a peaking block buy hypertrophy you do not need at the cost of Monday sprints.

TierMovementsWhat increasesTrigger
1 — Load Squat single · bench single · trap bar · weighted pull-up · front squat lbs on the bar Target RPE met with bar speed intact
2 — Velocity Speed squat · trap-bar jump · box & broad jump · plyo push-up · sandbag over-shoulder · explosive row / pull-up jump height, bar speed Mat cutoff ends the set — load stays fixed
3 — Held Leg press · leg extension · abduction · lat pulldown · hamstring curl · calf · lateral raise · curls · rotary torso · carries nothing, by design Only drift up if a set feels easy at RIR 2

The one place this could be wrong: if the Nov 2 1-minute test comes in flat and your jumps have not moved, the limiter is force, not rate — and Tier 3 should give up volume to feed Tier 1. That is a decision for the Week 8 deload, made on measured data, not now.
